## Single challenger and alternatives

| Choice | Storage, work, incentives | Judgment for owner |
|---|---|---|
| S: one bonded challenger + unlimited evidence events | O(1) single C and fixed terminal accounting; fixed window. A coalition owning proposer and first challenger keeps combined P+C in the candidate under any binary decision; other observers can submit evidence but cannot obtain challenger reward. Event gas and indexing cost remain. | Recommend retaining S for minimal v1 complexity **only if** owner explicitly accepts capture, funds independent monitoring and trusts adjudication. This is not an economic security guarantee. |
| M1: proportional challenger stake | Per-owner deposited principal and aggregate C; O(1) insertion and claim, no settlement loop. On reversal owner i receives C_i+floor(P*C_i/sum C); uphold awards P+sum C to proposer; neutral/timeout returns principals. Preserve rounding remainder as bond liability; zero sum must be rejected where a challenge is required. | If multiple economic challengers are required, prefer M1 to per-address voting. More capital can dilute honest rewards and a proposer coalition can recover P. Cap/minimum treatment and resulting exclusion must be decided and audited. |
| M2: fixed tranche per address | Count N and one fixed C per address; reversal pays C+floor(P/N), uphold P+N*C; neutral/timeout returns original deposits. O(1) claim without iteration, floor dust reserved. | A coalition can create cheap Sybil addresses and consume nearly all reward share. Do not adopt without explicit acceptance of this and per-address eligibility; address is not a person. A small global slot cap recreates exclusion. |

All alternatives need transparent indexer handling and gas budgets, no automatic payment, bounded user-directed claims and no promise that arbitrators consider submitted evidence. A participant or coalition may also hold YES/NO positions. A biased arbitrator can change participant payouts or choose INVALID to refund a losing stake; no bond allocation guarantees truthful adjudication. Timeout must refund principals even when arbitrator failed, rather than assume which actor was malicious.

## Adversarial arithmetic

[Independent local model](../../scripts/model-orakl-v1-economics.py), run as `python3 scripts/model-orakl-v1-economics.py > docs/economics/orakl-v1-model-output.json`, checks 81 matrix combinations (nine outcomes and P/C each 0, 1, or 101/103), 24 proportional stake/rounding cases and 12 simple challenger expected-return scenarios. [Exact output](orakl-v1-model-output.json). Counts and values are synthetic base units, not native QIE decimal or Mainnet gas observations. For fixed coalition total stake, splitting into multiple addresses cannot increase the sum of floor-proportional rewards; it can decrease it by rounding. Increasing total coalition stake still dilutes honest observers.

In the simplified binary-only model, challenger gross expected bond return is `q*P - (1-q)*C` before gas, monitoring, time and capital costs, with q the assumed probability that a challenge produces reversal. For P=C=5 and q=1/100, the expectation is `-49/10` units; at q=0 it is `-5`. INVALID, timeout and collusion are excluded from this formula, so this is not a production estimate. Detection probability can approach zero; proposal/challenge bonds then cannot guarantee challenges. A 10,000-unit external payoff against a 1,000-unit cap can exceed any internally modeled incentive. Economics cannot establish beneficial ownership or constrain bribes/hedges outside the contract.

No proposal/challenge principal may ever be funded from participant collateral. A policy that pays both P+C to one party conserves its deposited principal but can cause an incorrect good-faith party to lose principal. Candidate recipients and final INVALID behavior need explicit approval and independent review.
